You can spawn AI apps now

October 12, 2026 · spawnpoint

Updated in November 2026: the open models now run on Amazon Bedrock, in Amazon Web Services' Oregon region. Our account is set so that Amazon keeps none of your app's requests or answers, does not use them to train models, and does not share them with the companies that make the models. The model list below is the new one. Two models from October are gone. The search model bge-m3 has no replacement for now, so an app that searches your documents uses another company's search model, such as OpenAI's, with that company's key. Your agent makes the change and rebuilds the app's search index. The decision model laya is retired.

Your apps on spawnpoint can now use open-source AI models, with no provider account to make and no key to paste in. Open-source models cost a fraction of the closed models from the big labs to run. Try it: Runway is a choose-your-own-adventure through San Francisco startup life, written as you play by an open model on spawnpoint: Demo Day in 48 hours, a Series A pitch with a golden retriever in the room, a Friday outage with the one engineer who knows the deploy out of signal. Pick one of its three choices, or type what you do instead. The line under each scene shows how long the model took to write it.

Your agent decides when an app gets this, the way it decides when an app gets a database. An app it writes from scratch that needs a model uses an open model from the start. An app you already have that uses OpenAI or Anthropic is different, because you may have reasons to stay with them. Your agent asks you once: switch to an open model on spawnpoint, or keep what you have. If you keep it, nothing changes, and your agent does not ask again for that app. Each app that switches gets its own key to the models, created when the app goes live and deleted when the app is deleted. Right now there is no extra charge for the models. If that changes, we will say so first.

The models available today, and what each is for:

Model Good for
gpt-oss-120b Conversation, writing, summarizing, answering questions. The default, and the model for apps written for Anthropic's format.
gpt-oss-20b The same, faster, for apps that answer a lot of questions.
gemma-4-31b, gemma-4-26b Describing, reading, or comparing images, receipts, and scans.
mistral-large-3 Conversation in many languages.

Making images, speech, and video are not on the list yet, and neither is search over your own text. An app that does those keeps using the company it already uses for them, and your agent will tell you so.

We measured how long each model took to start answering, from our server in Oregon on November 7, in one test run: 0.19 seconds for mistral-large-3, 0.34 for gpt-oss-20b, 0.43 for gpt-oss-120b, and 0.46 for gemma-4-31b.

In October this post also described laya, a decision model that sorted text into categories in one pass. Amazon Bedrock has no model like it, so it is retired. A chat model does the same jobs, such as filing support messages to the right team or holding back spam, when your agent asks it to answer in a fixed format. It is slower than laya was.

The models run on Amazon Bedrock.
