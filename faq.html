FAQ

Common questions

What companies ask before their people start putting the apps they build with AI on spawnpoint. If your question is not here, write to support@getspawnpoint.com.

What spawnpoint is

What is spawnpoint?

The launchpad for your AI-enabled business. When you or someone at your company builds a tool, an app for customers, an MCP server, or an app that uses AI with a coding agent, spawnpoint puts it online with a private link. On a team, the app belongs to the company, and every member can open it. Nobody sets up or manages a server.

Who is it for?

Businesses whose people build with a coding agent such as Claude Code, Codex, or Cursor: companies that want their tools in one place the whole company can open, and founders, consultants, and agencies who launch apps for their customers. One person can use it too: a team is optional.

Can I build apps for my clients?

Yes. Consultants and agencies build apps for their clients, charge them for those apps, and run them on their own spawnpoint account. Share each one with the client's people by email, or with everyone at the client's company domain, and they open it with a code from their inbox and never make an account. You stay responsible for those apps under the Terms. What needs our written permission is reselling spawnpoint itself, such as selling spawnpoint accounts or offering spawnpoint as your own platform.

Do people need to know how to deploy?

No. Each person signs in once in the browser and their agent takes it from there. spawnpoint runs the app and gives it a secure (HTTPS) web address.

What can our agents build?

Any small web app: a page or a site, an app that keeps data (an inventory tracker, a lunch order form, a photo gallery), an app that uses AI, or an MCP server your team's agents share. The agent picks what the app needs, including a database or file storage, and spawnpoint provides it. Apps made with frameworks such as Next.js, Remix, Astro, and SvelteKit work, and so do apps in other languages: an app that comes with a Dockerfile is built on its own machine.

How does an agent connect?

You ask your agent to install spawnpoint. It sets up whichever coding agents are on the computer: Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Copilot, and many more. Send it this message:

Install spawnpoint for yourself: https://getspawnpoint.com/install

Then sign in once in the browser for each agent. On a team, send the agent the install line from the team page instead: it also adds the team's MCP servers and skills. The supported agents page has the exact step for each agent. Any other MCP client needs only https://app.getspawnpoint.com/mcp.

Teams

How does a team work?

Someone on Pro makes the team on the Team tab of the console. An admin invites people by email, or turns on domain join so that anyone who signs in with the same email domain joins it. Members do not need a plan of their own: the Pro plan of the person who made the team covers it, for up to 10 members. When a member connects an agent, they pick whether it deploys for them alone or for the team. Team apps show up on the team page with who built each one.

Who owns what the team builds?

The team does. An app an agent deploys for the team belongs to the team, not to the person who built it, so every member can open it and any admin can manage it. Admins add and remove members on the team page.

Can our agents share MCP servers and skills?

Yes. An MCP server is a small program that agents can ask for things, and a skill is a set of instructions for a task. A team's MCP servers are listed on its team page, and an agent can publish a skill to the team so every member's agent can follow it. The install line on the team page adds both to Claude Code and Codex in one step.

Sharing and safety

Who can see what we deploy?

Your team, and nobody else until you say so. A team app opens for every member, and a personal app for its builder alone. Share an app with specific people by email, or with everyone at a domain like @acme.com (they verify with a one-time code, no account needed), or make it public for anyone with the link. Agents can do all of this, and so can the console.

Can an app take payments?

Yes. Your agent can add Stripe to an app: a checkout page, subscriptions, and the messages Stripe sends back when a payment goes through. The Stripe keys go in the app's settings, which are encrypted, and never in its code: spawnpoint refuses to deploy an app with a Stripe key written into it. An app that receives Stripe's messages must be public, because Stripe cannot sign in to a private app.

Is it safe to deploy code an agent wrote?

spawnpoint refuses an app with a recognizable API key (AWS, Stripe, OpenAI, Anthropic, GitHub, and others) written into its code, and the plugin's security audit checks an app before it ships: leaked keys, files that should not upload, debug mode, and admin pages with no login. Every app with a server runs on its own isolated machine. On a team, only the member who built an app, or an admin, can delete it or its data.

Apps

Can our apps use AI without an API key?

Yes. spawnpoint gives apps open-source AI models to use. When an app needs AI, the agent asks whether to use them, and if you say yes, the app works with no OpenAI or Anthropic account and no key to paste in. The blog post You can spawn AI apps now shows what they can do.

Where do the open models run, and what is kept?

On Amazon Bedrock, which Amazon Web Services runs in the us-west-2 region (Oregon); requests are not sent to any other region. Our account is set to zero data retention, so Amazon Web Services keeps no requests or answers, does not use them to train models, and does not share them with the companies that make the models. spawnpoint passes each request and answer through and does not store them, keeping only a usage record for each call: the project, the model, the tokens, the cost, and the time. Embeddings, which an app uses to search its own text, run on Amazon Bedrock too, under the same terms. If you would rather use a model provider's own terms, use your own provider key. How apps run has the details.

Can we use our own domain?

Yes, on Pro and Business. Any app can have its own domain: ask your agent, and it gives you the DNS records to create. HTTPS is set up once spawnpoint confirms them. Every team app can also open under one domain your company owns, such as tracker.apps.yourcompany.com. The spawnpoint link keeps working too.

What happens when nobody is using an app?

It sleeps: the machine stops while it is idle, and the next visit wakes it in about half a minute. Redeploying also wakes it. On Pro, up to 5 apps can stay always on instead, so nobody waits. On Pro and Business, an app people usually open at the same time each day is started a few minutes before that time.

Can we take an app down?

Yes. Open it in the console and click Terminate, or ask your agent. The app is permanently shut down. On a team, the member who built it or an admin can do this.

Can a private app receive webhooks?

Yes. Your agent lists the paths the webhook arrives at, such as the one a Slack event or a HubSpot webhook is sent to, and anyone can reach those paths while the rest of the app stays private. You can also add them in the Public paths box on the app's page in the console. The app checks the sender's signature on those paths itself. How apps run has the details.

Does my app need its own login?

Usually not. spawnpoint already decides who can open an app: your team, or the people and company domains you share it with, who sign in with a code from their inbox. A private app is also told who each viewer is, so it can show each client their own records or let only one person publish, with no login of its own. An app needs a sign-in of its own only when it must be public, for example to receive webhooks, or must let in people who are not on its share list. Then we recommend Auth0, which has quickstarts for Express, Next.js, Python, and React.

Can an app run jobs on a schedule?

Yes, inside the app. Run the scheduler in the app's own process, such as node-cron in Node or APScheduler in Python, next to a web server on PORT, and keep the app always on, because a sleeping app's schedule stops. Pro includes 5 always-on apps. There is no built-in scheduler yet. How apps run covers background work.

What happens to a running job when I redeploy?

The new version installs and builds while the running one keeps serving. Then the running version receives SIGTERM and has 20 seconds to finish, and the new one starts. A failed install or build stops nothing, and a failed start puts the old version back. A job that can run longer than 20 seconds should save its progress and resume. How apps run explains redeploys and migrations.

Can I move an app to another account?

Not directly yet. A personal app can move into your team, but an app cannot move from one account to another. To move one, export its database, deploy the same code under the other account, restore the database there, and point any custom domain at the new app. An agent can do each step. How apps run lists them.

Pricing and billing

How much does it cost?

Free is for trying an idea on your own: one live app at a time, sharing with up to 50 people per app, and $5 a month of AI usage on open models. Pro is $100 a month: unlimited apps, up to 2 teams with up to 10 members each, and 5 apps that never sleep. Only the person who makes a team pays: the members they invite don't. Business adds an engineer who helps build your first apps. Pro is a flat monthly subscription with nothing to top up, managed or cancelled in the console. The pricing page compares the plans.

How does billing work?

Pro is a flat monthly subscription: $100 a month for each person who subscribes, with nothing to top up and nothing billed by usage. You start it in the console, and one checkout covers your own apps and up to 2 teams you make. Invoices, receipts, your card, and cancelling are in Stripe's billing portal, which the console opens for you.

What happens to my apps if I cancel?

Your account goes back to Free's limits, and nothing is deleted. The apps you have keep running, and your agent can still update them. Free runs one live app at a time, so a new app waits until you terminate the ones you have or subscribe again. Always-on apps go back to sleeping when idle, a database larger than 256 MiB turns read-only, and file storage over 500 MiB stops taking new files. Teams you made keep their apps and members, and new apps and invites on them wait until a Pro plan covers them again.

Do my teammates need Pro?

No. The person who makes the team pays, and the people they invite don't. One Pro plan covers up to 2 teams its subscriber makes, each with up to 10 members. A member's personal apps, outside the team, follow their own plan.
